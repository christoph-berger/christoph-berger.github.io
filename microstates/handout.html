EEG Microstates: A Compact View of Topographic Dynamics

Six key points · handout / poster companion

1

Recurring topographies

EEG scalp maps change continuously, but certain spatial configurations recur. Around moments of high global field power (GFP), these dominant topographies can be especially well defined and reproducible.

2

A low-dimensional summary

Microstate analysis clusters similar scalp topographies into a small number of representative classes. It provides a low-dimensional description of the continuously evolving global EEG field.

3

Dominant, not exclusive

A microstate class reflects the currently dominant topographic configuration. It does not imply that the whole brain is in a single mutually exclusive physiological state; multiple generators and networks may contribute in parallel.

4

Temporal dynamics become measurable

Discretization turns ongoing topographic fluctuation into a symbolic sequence such as A → C → B → D, enabling analysis of duration, occurrence, coverage, transitions, syntax, entropy and higher-order temporal structure.

5

Only part of the EEG state

The class label mainly captures spatial configuration. Other relevant features – amplitude, spectral content, phase, connectivity and the precise mixture of neural generators – are not fully represented by the label alone.

6

A useful coarse-grained representation

Microstates are neither arbitrary labels nor complete physiological states. They are recurring dominant configurations of the global EEG field that provide a compact, physiologically meaningful representation of brain dynamics.

Colours mark microstate classes consistently across panels: A B C D

Take-home message: Microstates capture dominant recurring configurations of the EEG field – a useful coarse-grained representation of brain dynamics, rather than a complete description of the underlying physiological state.
